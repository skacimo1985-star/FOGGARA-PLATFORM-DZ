# FOGGARA — primary-source legal and engineering register

Checked: 2026-09-30. Scope: engineering design and release review. This register is not a legal opinion, exhaustive consolidation, certification, or evidence that a deployment complies. The actual data flows, operator, contracts, hosting, sector and applicable implementing texts must be reviewed before production. No real user dataset was sent to research providers for this review.

## Algerian texts verified

| Reference and date | Official source | Verified scope and design consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Law 18-07, 10 June 2018, JO 34 | [JORADP, pp. 10–19](https://www.joradp.dz/FTP/JO-FRANCAIS/2018/F2018034.pdf) | Personal data: purpose, minimisation and retention (Art. 9); prior declaration/authorisation procedures (Art. 12 onward); information/access/rectification/opposition (Arts. 32–36); risk-appropriate security and processor contracts (Arts. 38–39). Art. 7 includes express consent and enumerated exceptions: do not label every processing activity as consent-based. Foreign transfers require assessing Arts. 44–45, rather than assuming consent or encryption alone resolves them. Art. 43 requires notification without delay in its electronic-network-service context; Art. 38 is not a universal 72-hour rule. |
| Law 25-11, 24 July 2025, JO 48 | [JORADP, pp. 14–18](https://www.joradp.dz/FTP/jo-francais/2025/F2025048.pdf) | Amends 18-07; adds profiling, biometrics, pseudonymisation and breach definitions. Art. 41 bis provides for a data-protection delegate and authority contact; Art. 41 bis 2 describes controller/processor processing registers; Art. 41 bis 3 describes automated operation logging with purpose, time and actors/recipients where possible. Implementing rules remain relevant. The new Title V bis, including impact/breach provisions, concerns criminal-law processing by competent authorities: do not copy its duties into generic SaaS without applicability review. Original Arts. 10–11 are expressly repealed. Human approval remains FOGGARA's safety/product policy, not a claim based on repealed Art. 11. |
| Law 26-02, 17 February 2026, JO 14 of 18 February 2026 | [Official MPT-hosted JO extract](https://www.mpt.gov.dz/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/Loi-n%C2%B0-26-02.Services-de-Confiance.Identification-electronique.FR_.pdf) | Trust services and electronic identification. Art. 115 repeals Law 15-04, while retaining its implementing texts until replacements; Arts. 109–113 provide transitions. A document hash, JWT, staff review or audit event is not automatically a qualified signature, trust service or verified legal identity. Integration must use the applicable authorised trust-service framework. |
| Law 18-05, 10 May 2018, JO 28 of 16 May 2018 | [JORADP, pp. 4–9](https://www.joradp.dz/FTP/JO-FRANCAIS/2018/F2018028.pdf) | Electronic commerce. Art. 8 sets registration and Algerian hosting/.com.dz conditions for e-commerce; Art. 9 addresses domain submission; offer, contract, payment and consumer duties require review if these functions are enabled. A collaboration demonstrator is not proof that commercial operation has been authorised. |
| Law 09-04, 5 August 2009, JO 47 of 16 August 2009 | [Official MPT-hosted JO extract](https://www.mpt.gov.dz/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/Loi-n%C2%B0-09-04-fr.pdf) | ICT-related offences; Arts. 10–11 address service-provider assistance and defined traffic retention, including one year for specified data. Counsel must determine FOGGARA's provider category and applicable implementing rules. Do not retain every document or sensitive observation for one year merely by citing this provision; traffic records and operational content are distinct. |
| Presidential Decree 20-05, 20 January 2020 | [Official MPT-hosted JO extract](https://www.mpt.gov.dz/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/Decret-presidentiel-n%C2%B0-20-05.fr_.pdf) | National information-system security framework. It is a presidential decree, not an executive decree. Sector/organisation-specific requirements and engagement with competent bodies must be assessed for public or sensitive infrastructure work. |
| Presidential Decree 26-07, 7 January 2026 | [Official ARPCE notice](https://www.arpce.dz/fr/pub/w6h8s2) | Establishes an information-system security/data-protection structure in institutions, administrations and public bodies. This scope must not be presented as an identical organisational duty imposed automatically on a private EURL; public-sector deployments need their own contractual and authority review. |

Additional regulator reference: [ANPDP's own current privacy notice](https://plaintes.anpdp.dz/notice.php) expressly references 18-07 as amended by 25-11. Its retention, hosting and contact statements describe ANPDP's processing, not FOGGARA's deployment.

## Release work and accountable decisions

The following are project engineering requirements derived from the above risk review; they are not claims that all are already implemented:

1. Maintain a processing register per purpose: controller/processor role, lawful basis, categories, recipients, transfers, retention and security. Provide versioned notices, separate optional publication consent and accessible rights-request handling.
2. Keep sensitive personal, geospatial and infrastructure data out of public demos and foreign AI calls. Evaluate hosting, backups, logs, analytics, support access and model inference as actual flows. Algerian hosting is a project requirement for sensitive production data; the legal transfer review is more nuanced than a universal localisation assertion.
3. Authorise every read, mutation, export and AI retrieval by organisation and object. Log access to controlled disclosures with minimal metadata; never log passwords, bearer tokens or entire documents. Protect logs and reconcile retention with legal holds and rights.
4. Classify measurements as observed, reviewed, computed, inferred or proposed. Preserve provenance and algorithm version. Empty states remain empty: no invented projects, locations, certifications, values or connectivity status.
5. Maintain incident discovery/escalation/notification records and a responsible contact. Applicable notification deadlines and recipients must be approved from the correct legal scope, not imported from GDPR defaults.

Before collecting production data, management and its qualified legal/DPO advisers must validate the ANPDP procedure, designation/contact requirements, actual lawful bases and retention, processor contracts and transfers, sector permissions, commerce applicability and trust-service integration. Security operations must validate hardened hosting, backups/restoration, keys, monitoring and an independent penetration test. Laboratory accreditation, legal compliance and sovereign deployment each require external evidence; passing software tests cannot establish them.

Review limitation: this source check verified the listed official texts and recent amendments; it does not establish that every later implementing text or sector rule has been located. Re-check JORADP/ANPDP and competent sector authorities before any production release.
